01What a buyer is really buying

As a buyer, I buy what I would have built anyway. The company has to save my team time and focus, close a gap my customers already feel, and keep running without its founder.

So the easiest company to sell is one the buyer was already planning to build.

02The 5 things a buyer checks before making an offer

In this order. The right column is what I think when I sit in the buyer's chair.

What you need to doWhat I think as a buyer
2.1Be a founder the buyer trustsGet to know the buyers in your space long before you need them."It starts with a connection to people I trust."
2.2Show what the buyer can fixKnow what a buyer could improve in your company. Obvious upside is easier to buy."Either improving profitability or improving the product."
2.3Fill a gap in the buyer's productSay in one sentence which hole you close."A complementary product that fills the holes."
2.4Build systems that run without youA buyer pays for a business that keeps working the day you step back."Make sure the business is sustainable even without you."
2.5Be on the buyer's roadmapFind the buyer who was already planning to build what you have."We don't buy something we don't know. Usually it's something we planned to build anyway."

03What a buyer looks for in you

Your company is half the deal. The other half is you, the person the buyer will hit targets with for the next few years. When I buy, this is what I look for.

  1. I trust you, ideally through people I already know.
  2. You tell the truth, including the weak parts, in the first conversation.
  3. You sign up for targets and believe we hit them together.
  4. You know your numbers by heart.
  5. You still want to play the game after the deal closes.

04What to bring to the first meeting

Come with these 7 and you skip the getting-to-know-you meeting.

  1. Your monthly profit for the last 12 months.
  2. The fixes list. What a buyer could improve, and what your profit looks like after.
  3. One sentence on the hole you close in the buyer's product.
  4. The targets you would sign up for after the deal.
  5. Your plan for the team on the day after.
  6. A name on their side who knows you and trusts you.
  7. A deck built like the one that sold my company. The Exit Deck template, free.

06Do's and don'ts if you want to sell

Do

  • Get to know the buyers in your space years before you sell.
  • Practice on buyers you would never sell to, and fix the slide that loses the room.
  • Build systems so the business runs without you.
  • Ask every buyer what happens to your team the day after.
  • Show the targets you would sign up for.

Don't

  • Pitch your dream buyer first.
  • Pretend the buyer has no alternative. If they are entering your space anyway, say it.
  • Build a business only to flip it.
  • Sell to a buyer whose plan is to squeeze the company for cash.
  • Let a deal drag for months. My exit closed in 23 days.

077 questions to ask a buyer before you sign

I've sat on both sides of the table. Two years before my exit I walked away from a buyer a day before closing, because his plan was to cut the team that built the company with me. These are the questions that would have saved me 6 months.

  1. What happens to my team the day after?
  2. Is your plan to grow the company or to squeeze it for cash?
  3. Which targets will I be measured on, and what happens if we miss one?
  4. Why buy me instead of building it yourself?
  5. How fast can you close?
  6. Who on your side will I work with every day?
  7. Why now?

08What made the companies I bought worth buying

The rival I knew better than anyone

One company I bought was the market leader when I started. They had everything I did not have at 21. I spent years studying their product and their customers, overtook them, then bought them. By the time we talked, I knew exactly what I was buying. The lesson for you: the buyer who knows your product best will pay for it with the least doubt.

The company that opened a new market

Another company had built a community, something that takes years and money to build, and it sold to customers I did not reach. Buying it added no revenue on day one. It opened a market I was not in. The lesson for you: your value to a buyer can be a door, and the revenue comes after.

What both had: something I could not build fast. Know what yours is.

09What the buyer needs from you at the table

Every deal I do runs on targets. So the question I need you to answer is simple: do you trust that together, we hit them?

  1. Show me the targets you would sign up for, and how we hit them together.
  2. Show me why selling to me pays you more than running alone.
  3. Be honest about the alternative. Sometimes the seller already knows I'm entering their space anyway. A founder who sees the whole picture decides faster.

10My 3 biggest mistakes, and what they mean for you

  1. I wasn't aggressive enough. The deals I regret are the ones I skipped. Same playbook, I knew what I was doing, and I should have taken a loan to close. For you: buyers hesitate too. Make the yes easy.
  2. I didn't make the time. Some deals I passed on because I didn't have the time to do them properly. For you: a buyer with no time is a buyer who stalls. Ask who on their side owns the deal.
  3. I spent 6 months with the wrong buyer. As a seller, I learned the buyer's plan for my team only at the end, and walked away a day before closing. For you: ask about the plan on the first call.
